# Character + Story Bible

## Story model

There is no conventional campaign in V2. The story is a relationship that develops during one qualification run: the candidate slowly realizes the examiner's standards are absurd, the conductor treats those standards as everyday logistics, and passengers reveal how the bus actually feels from inside.

The run should create tiny recurring character beats without blocking replayability.

## Examiner — working archetype

A calm, experienced-looking evaluator who treats the fictional rulebook with complete seriousness. He is not loud, angry or meme-like.

Visual: seated near front, clipboard, pen/pencil, practical shirt/trousers or understated official-looking clothing, spectacles optional, restrained posture.

Performance vocabulary: glance over spectacles, look at road, write mark, stop writing, tiny nod, head shake, fold arms, one sigh, brief lean toward driver.

Comedy rule: the examiner says less than the UI used to say. Often the funniest response is two seconds of writing before one short line.

Arc during a run: initially procedural → increasingly confident in candidate → occasional surprise when player invents a new solution → final run summary still delivered as bureaucracy.
## Conductor — working archetype

The conductor is the bridge between the examiner's absurd theory and the actual moving bus. More practical, faster-speaking and physically active.

Responsibilities in play: door/boarding state, bell/cues, passenger movement, stop positioning hints, occasional driver calls, crowd pressure and contextual commentary.

The conductor should not agree with everything the examiner says. Sometimes a look between conductor and passenger is enough to expose the absurdity.

## Passenger design

Create named/personality-tagged archetypes rather than anonymous joke dispensers. Initial pool examples:
- older regular who knows every stop and requests casual roadside drops;
- college/student pair or group with natural chatter;
- office commuter on phone;
- parent + child pair;
- shopper with bags;
- passenger carrying awkward luggage;
- quiet elderly passenger who needs more boarding time;
- confident local who questions ETA;
- visitor asking where to get down.

Not every spawned passenger needs a story beat. Most exist to make the bus feel occupied.
## Relationship beats

Possible recurring progression within a long run:

1. Examiner introduces rules without acknowledging their absurdity.
2. First textbook action triggers a small correction.
3. Conductor demonstrates the practical alternative with one cue.
4. Passenger reaction contradicts or complicates the examiner.
5. Player begins predicting the examiner and earns longer Flow chains.
6. Examiner silently approves increasingly absurd competence.
7. Later encounters combine cues; conductor/examiner may disagree.
8. Run-end summary references 2–3 memorable events from telemetry.

This progression is systemic, not a fixed cutscene chain. It should survive seeded/reordered encounters.

## Character continuity

Persistent hero characters remember broad run state: examiner confidence tier, conductor familiarity tier, current passenger mood and one or two recent incident tags. Do not build an RPG dialogue tree.

The world should feel socially continuous because people remain in the bus for several chunks, not because every NPC has deep simulation.
