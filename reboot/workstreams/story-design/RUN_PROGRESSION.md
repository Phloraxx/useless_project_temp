# Run Progression — Authored Opening into Endless Route

## Run philosophy

The first ten minutes teach the player three things without tutorial cards: the bus has weight, the social world has readable cues, and the examiner’s doctrine is absurd but learnable. After that, the game stops introducing mandatory story beats and lets the same systems recombine indefinitely.

The authored route is not a separate level. It is a controlled first segment of the same chunk streamer. On repeat runs, mandatory exposition shortens automatically while the key mechanical beats remain.

## 0:00–1:15 — Depot office / boarding

**Setup:** application slip, duplicate stamp, examiner checks credentials, conductor reports missing scheduled driver. Test and service are merged.

**Player action:** enter/start bus, observe boarding, release brake and depart on conductor clear.

**World reaction:** depot remains busy; nobody treats the situation as cinematic destiny.

**Examiner:** procedural, almost no humor in delivery.

**Conductor/passengers:** conductor is focused on departure; passengers ask ordinary route questions over the briefing.

**Scoring:** first smooth pull-away gives a tiny Flow seed; unsafe door-open departure is blocked/penalized.

**Camera/audio:** diegetic office framing → exterior bus reveal → chase; stamps, fan, diesel idle, footsteps, bell, air brake.

**Callback:** “administrative efficiency,” wrong/right stamp, examiner’s pen.

## 1:15–2:15 — Learn the bus before the joke

A short residential/open stretch with no punchline. Mild bend, one speed breaker, enough distance to feel steering, throttle and braking. Examiner watches silently.

The only goal is good driving. Smoothness can raise Flow to x1.5/x2. This proves the game is not waiting for dialogue to become fun.

## 2:15–3:10 — First perfect-stop reveal

Deterministic marked stop. Safe practical window is broad; the painted ideal point is deliberately conspicuous.

If player stops exactly: passengers board fine, then examiner quietly marks “over-prepared / textbook tendency.” If player stops within a sensible practical band: small Aptitude bonus. If player stops badly or dangerously: ordinary failure, no inversion.

Use the strongest first grading cinematic here because it teaches the premise once.## 3:10–4:20 — Slow boarder under pressure

An older passenger boards slowly while traffic stacks behind. The player must wait for the conductor’s actual clear signal. Rear traffic horns create pressure but are not a command.

This beat teaches the hard safety boundary: the examiner’s weirdness never overrides vulnerable-passenger safety. A smooth restart after the bell earns back Flow.

Small character beat: examiner starts to write during the delay, then stops when he realizes there is nothing legitimate to mark.

## 4:20–5:20 — Safe decisiveness

Side-road vehicle / ordinary merge with an obvious but finite safe opportunity. Either a decisive yield or decisive proceed can succeed; repeated indecision loses a little Flow and may earn examiner disapproval.

This teaches that “confidence” means readable commitment, not aggression.

Conductor should not speak unless necessary. Passenger chatter continuing through the maneuver makes the world feel less staged.

## 5:20–6:20 — Passenger request + ETA texture

A passenger asks about a destination, another passenger supplies an estimate, conductor gives a practical answer, examiner listens too seriously. Shortly after, a stop request arrives.

The player’s real task is remembering the upcoming stop and setting up early enough without panic braking. The social comedy occupies the same seconds as ordinary route planning.

## 6:20–7:20 — First weather or roughness escalation

Prefer a light monsoon onset on first-run showcase seed; alternate runs may use rough road instead. Wipers, wet tire sound and passenger movement become new information channels.

A speed breaker / patched surface tests whether the player can preserve Flow while conditions change. No joke is required.

## 7:20–8:30 — Rulebook gap

Combine a legitimate stop request with roadworks or a puddled/blocked shoulder. The formal stop point is unusable, but a nearby safe drop exists.

The player who chooses the practical safe point triggers the examiner’s first real pause. He cannot find the box, then invents/reclassifies a clause and awards strong Aptitude. Conductor notices; passenger simply gets off.

This is the story turn: the player is no longer only learning the examiner. The examiner begins learning how to describe the player.## 8:30–10:00 — First combined stretch

Use one medium-density chunk with two compatible pressures: standing passengers plus curves, or market queue plus next-stop request. The player should now be able to build a meaningful Flow chain without a tutorial.

Examiner reactions shrink to glances and nods. Conductor cues become shorter. A passenger from earlier can produce the first callback. End the ten-minute authored segment on motion, not a cutscene.

## 10:00 onward — Seamless endless score attack

There is no banner saying “ENDLESS MODE.” The Encounter Director simply unlocks the full common/uncommon pool, broader chunk sequencing, stronger modifier combinations and rare-event chance.

Story state continues systemically:
- examiner confidence rises with successful novel resolutions;
- conductor familiarity shortens cues and changes reaction pools;
- passenger manifest churn creates new social combinations;
- incident memory stores 2–3 tags for callbacks/result text;
- weather and district/theme seeds alter familiar encounter reads.

The authored arc can reach a certificate checkpoint at a safe terminus after roughly 15–25 minutes, depending on run quality and pacing. This is not a hard end. The player may accept the result and finish, or continue for score and distance.## Escalation structure

### Tier 0 — Orientation, 0–2 min
One pressure at a time. Low traffic. No rare events. Wide scoring windows.

### Tier 1 — Reading the institution, 2–5 min
Introduce fictional preference beats: practical stop placement, decisive safe merge, contextual horn communication. Still no stacked ambiguity.

### Tier 2 — Social bus, 5–10 min
Passenger requests, boarding delays, standing load, route questions, weather onset. Up to two compatible pressures.

### Tier 3 — Competence, 10–18 min
More uncommon encounters, denser traffic, reduced telegraph generosity, persistent rain/roughness, and conductor cues overlapping normal chatter. Never hide safety-critical information.

### Tier 4 — Improvisation, 18–30 min
Rulebook-gap encounters, three-layer situations only when one layer is ambient rather than decision-critical. Rare events become possible. Examiner uses prior incident callbacks.

### Tier 5 — Endless mastery, 30+ min
Difficulty grows through sequencing, recovery demands, passenger load and changing conditions—not simply higher speed. Maintain quiet stretches. Long Flow chains become the aspirational challenge.

## Pacing constraints

Target one meaningful decision every 10–25 seconds, but protect 20–45 second quiet stretches after dense combinations. Never fire two examiner cinematics back-to-back. After a major passenger/social beat, prefer vehicle-feel or environmental play next.

Every five minutes should ideally contain: at least one pure driving beat, one passenger/conductor beat, one examiner-grade beat, one environmental observation, and one quiet stretch. This is a pacing budget, not a checklist visible to the player.
