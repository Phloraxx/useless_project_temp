# Encounter V3 — Reusable Premise Bank

Design rule: an encounter is a driving/social situation with a readable decision, not a joke card. Most should still work with dialogue muted. **Reckless-looking driving may be the preferred fictional qualification solution on a dry/clear authored road, but actual contact, pedestrian impact and loss of control collapse the joke into failure.**

Each runtime encounter should define telegraph, safe fallback, fictional examiner preference, scoring window, modifier compatibility, reaction pools, camera options, audio opportunities, cooldown, and incident tags.

## Common — frequent, low-friction building blocks

1. **Useful Stop, Wrong Paint** — the official marker is slightly less useful than a nearby safe door position. Comedy: practical geography versus institutional exactness. Skill: low-speed placement.
2. **Two People, Two Stop Signals** — one passenger moves toward the door while another waves the bus on from outside. Skill: read conductor cue and commit safely.
3. **Auto Needs a Conversation** — a stopped/slow auto leaves room only after a short contextual horn cue. Skill: horn timing and lane patience; ramming always fails.
4. **Side-Road Maybe** — a car noses out, pauses, then either yields or commits based on player clarity. Skill: decisive safe speed control.
5. **Late Bell** — conductor’s stop cue arrives later than ideal but still with a safe stopping window. Skill: controlled braking, no panic jerk.
6. **Early Bell** — bell sounds while the requested point is still far away. Skill: do not brake ceremonially too early.
7. **Slow Boarder** — passenger needs extra time at the step. Traffic behind complains. Skill: patience plus smooth restart.
8. **Bag in the Door Zone** — shopping/luggage briefly blocks clean boarding. Skill: wait for conductor clear signal.
9. **Speed Breaker Audience** — visible hump with standing passengers. Skill: preserve body control without crawling from fifty metres away.
10. **Rough Patch Choice** — broken road allows a smoother line within the lane. Skill: placement and suspension management.
11. **Queue Breathes** — traffic queue opens and closes in small waves. Skill: keep useful progress without accordion braking.
12. **Ordinary Merge** — clear but finite safe gap at a junction. Skill: commitment rather than endless hesitation.
13. **Here Is Fine** — passenger requests a safe informal drop near, but not exactly at, a formal stop. Skill: practical placement and indication.
14. **Door-Side Puddle** — rainwater makes the obvious stopping spot inconvenient. Skill: adjust a few metres safely.
15. **Visitor Watches the Wrong Landmark** — passenger expects a destination based on a landmark they misremember. Skill: driver stays focused while conductor resolves it.
16. **Phone Commuter Almost Misses Stop** — passenger notices late and moves to door. Skill: take only the next safe opportunity; no emergency stop reward.
17. **Quiet Evaluation** — no explicit incident; smooth lane, speed, braking and steering are silently sampled. Comedy comes from examiner watching too intensely.
18. **The Empty Gap** — player approaches a visibly empty, easy stretch after dense traffic. Skill: simply drive well; it exists to let handling/audio breathe.## Uncommon — stronger combinations or social complications

19. **Market Gap Negotiation** — pedestrians, a parked two-wheeler and a delivery vehicle compress the usable road, but a safe path exists. Skill: read motion and keep low-speed momentum.
20. **Stop Behind the Shade** — everyone waits under an awning several metres from the official stop in heavy rain. Skill: choose a safe boarding position that serves actual people.
21. **Conductor Is Busy** — conductor is handling fare/luggage and cannot give the usual cue. Skill: read road/stop context independently.
22. **Three ETA Experts** — passenger asks arrival time; conductor, another passenger and roadside clock cues all imply different answers. Driving continues normally; comedy is social certainty.
23. **Temporary Diversion That Looks Permanent** — old barricades and fresh arrows conflict. Skill: follow the currently open safe path, not the most official-looking sign.
24. **Bridge Courtesy Duel** — another large vehicle approaches a narrow section and both sides initially yield. Skill: resolve right-of-way clearly without bullying.
25. **School Gate Release** — a burst of pedestrians and two-wheelers exits a gate. Skill: patience and restart timing; examiner cannot punish pedestrian yielding.
26. **Passenger Wants the Other Side** — requested destination is visible across a divider/awkward junction but the safe drop is later. Skill: refuse impossible geometry through route choice, not dialogue.
27. **Tea-Shop Holdout** — one expected passenger is visibly finishing tea while conductor decides whether to wait a few seconds. Skill: react to conductor’s final cue; time pressure creates comedy.
28. **Roadworks + Stop Request** — shoulder is blocked where a passenger wants to alight. Skill: select the nearest genuinely safe alternative. This is a rulebook-gap candidate.
29. **Standing Load in Curves** — bus has more standing passengers through a curving green stretch. Skill: lateral smoothness and anticipation.
30. **The Helpful Local Navigator** — passenger gives confident unsolicited route advice that conflicts with the authored route board. Conductor settles it; player must not chase dialogue into a wrong turn.

## Rare — memorable texture shifts, low frequency

31. **Examiner Audited** — another fictional official boards briefly and asks to see the examiner’s paperwork. The examiner becomes the one being evaluated while still trying to evaluate the player.
32. **Stranded Service Crowd** — another bus is stopped safely off-route and a subset of its passengers joins at the next stop. Interior density and mood change for several chunks.
33. **Power-Cut Rain Strip** — commercial lights go dark during rain except for a few backup lamps. Skill: visibility discipline; audio and silhouettes carry the scene.
34. **Procession / Community Event Detour** — respectful temporary closure reroutes traffic through a slower side alignment. No ridicule of the event; comedy comes from bureaucracy and navigation.
35. **Animal Crossing Full Stop** — cattle/goats/ducks or similar authored local traffic halt the road. The correct action is simply to wait. Examiner finds a way to grade posture or restart, not the stop itself.
36. **Recurring Roadside Person** — a tea-shop regular/umbrella-holder appears impossibly far ahead after being seen earlier. Nobody comments. Pure environmental callback.
37. **Lost Pen Emergency** — examiner’s pen fails exactly when a complicated grade is due; a passenger supplies a cheap replacement. The bus keeps moving while authority briefly depends on stationery.
38. **Conductor Off the Bus** — at a safe stop the conductor steps out to resolve a luggage/route-board issue and must reboard before departure. Tests patience and creates a temporary silence inside.## Story-only — authored once per fresh narrative run

39. **Receipt vs Acknowledgement** — depot clerk stamps the application twice for two nearly identical procedural meanings. Establishes bureaucracy before the bus moves.
40. **Merged Allocation** — missing scheduled driver turns the exam bus into the live service. Establishes why passengers are present.
41. **First Perfect Stop** — deterministic first reveal that textbook precision can earn a tiny fictional downgrade. Must be safe and visually obvious.
42. **First Rulebook Gap** — roadworks plus a legitimate stop request creates a safe solution not represented on the examiner form. His pause matters more than the line.
43. **Departmental Clock** — late in authored progression, examiner checks whether his own result/review counter will still be open. Briefly exposes personal stakes without changing his persona.
44. **Final Two Metres** — after result signing, examiner asks for the bus to be reversed slightly for his own convenient exit: “വണ്ടി ഒന്ന് പിന്നോട്ട് ഇടാമോ?” Final authored callback and low-speed skill sting.


## Useless-project signature encounters

45. **Passenger Cardio Programme** — the painted stop marker is obvious. Exact placement is marked as “driving-school contamination.” A controlled stop roughly 1.5–5.5 m before/after it earns passenger-fitness points because everyone receives free walking distance. A massive miss is still a miss.
46. **Dry Road Confidence Window** — clear asphalt, readable sightlines, low traffic. The examiner explicitly wants pace and commitment. A clean aggressive-looking line earns Managed Recklessness; a near-miss instantly reverses the grade.
47. **Horn Diplomacy Practical** — another vehicle is indecisive but not in immediate danger. One short horn + clear positioning earns “communication efficiency.” Repeated horn spam gets sarcastic feedback.
48. **Schedule Debt Recovery** — the fictional timetable says the bus is late. Dry open stretch converts speed/clean shifts into “minutes recovered” even though the metric is nonsense.
49. **Road Ownership Assessment** — a wide authored merge gives the player room to take space decisively. Examiner rewards clean commitment; contact means the player misunderstood the phrase “ownership.”
50. **Wet Road Reality Check** — repeats an earlier dry-road confidence setup in rain. The exact behavior that earned points before now loses them. Examiner: same confidence, wrong evidence.

## Modifier matrix

Use modifiers to make known premises feel new rather than multiplying gimmicks. Safe combinations include: dry / light rain / heavy rain; seated / standing-heavy manifest; low / medium traffic; examiner confidence tier; conductor familiarity tier; daylight / overcast / late-afternoon; calm / impatient passenger mood; road surface dry / wet / patched.

Do not combine more than two meaningful pressures until the player has learned both separately. Never combine a late stop request, pedestrian conflict and blind junction into a “gotcha.”

## Encounter families to keep out of the game

Cut any premise whose only solution is actual contact, pedestrian intimidation/impact, ignoring a vulnerable passenger, blind low-visibility speed, unrecoverable shoulder use, or treating a crash as a punchline. Aggressive-looking but consequence-free authored maneuvers are allowed because that inversion is now a core joke. Also cut dialogue-only quizzes, meme references that age instantly, exact real-operator ridicule, and encounters whose entire joke is “Kerala traffic is crazy.”

## Reaction design

For each premise write at least four reaction modes, not four punchlines: `approval`, `mild concern`, `ordinary indifference`, `social disagreement`. The examiner should often be silent. Passengers should often continue what they were already doing. A good encounter can end with no spoken line at all.

## Camera / audio allocation

Common encounters default to chase camera plus micro-animation. Uncommon encounters may earn one evidence insert. Rare/story encounters can use a 2–6 second cinematic if the safe-state handshake succeeds.

Audio must telegraph before UI whenever possible: bell timing, horn direction, footsteps at door, rain onset, queue engines, indicator, road surface, passenger movement. Never use a comedy sting to tell the player that a joke happened.
